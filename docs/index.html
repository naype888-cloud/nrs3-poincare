Only sharp levels give Planck's law

Build a density of states: sharp levels at nε, smeared by σ, plus a continuous part of weight α. The mean energy at temperature 1/β is computed exactly from the Laplace transform. Poincaré proved in 1912, and Lean checks, that Planck's ε/(eβε − 1) at every temperature forces σ = 0, α = 0.

Exact formulas (ε = 1): smeared levels Z = eβ²σ²/2/(1 − e−β), U = 1/(eβ − 1) − βσ²; continuum Z = 1/β, U = 1/β; mixtures add the Z's. Gaussian tails reach below E = 0, so the mean can turn negative when cold; the theorem allows any real energies.
